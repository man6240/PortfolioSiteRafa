Privacy policy

Effective 3 October 2026 · Applies to Sugoi Fruit Merge: Watermelon on Android

The short version

  • There's no account and no sign-in. You never give me your email, real name or phone number.
  • Your best score, seeds and settings are saved only on your phone. Nothing about your game is stored on my servers.
  • The game shows ads through Google AdMob. Where the law requires it, you're asked before ads are personalised, and you can change your mind from Settings.
  • Purchases go through Google Play. I never see your card or bank details.
  • Daily reminders are optional and scheduled on your phone. I don't sell your data.

Who I am

Sugoi Fruit Merge: Watermelon is made by Rafael Alejandro Vitriago Rivero, an independent game developer living in Spain who publishes as Baldman Studios. I'm the data controller for the personal data described here, which means I decide how it's used and I'm responsible for protecting it.

Contact: rafaelrivero6240@gmail.com. I'm a one-person studio, so there's no data protection officer — questions come straight to me.

This policy follows the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and Spain's data protection law (LOPDGDD). Because the game is played worldwide, it also covers the rights you have under other laws, such as the UK GDPR, the California Consumer Privacy Act and Brazil's LGPD. Whatever the law where you live, I give every player the rights described here.

Ads

The game is free, and ads pay for it. They're served by Google AdMob. Some are short videos you choose to watch in exchange for seeds or a second chance. Others appear now and then between games — at most about once a minute.

To show and measure ads, AdMob and the ad partners it works with receive the device and ad data listed above. Google and each ad partner decide for themselves how they use that data, so each one is responsible for it too. You can read more in how Google uses information from apps that use its services.

Your choices

  • In the EU, UK and Switzerland, the game shows Google's consent form before ads are personalised to you, with a clear option not to consent. You can change your answer later with the Privacy button in the game's Settings.
  • Everywhere, you can delete or reset your advertising ID in Android's settings (Settings → Privacy → Ads, or Google → Ads on some phones). Ads won't stop, but they'll no longer be based on your interests.
  • If you live in a US state with its own privacy law, such as California: personalised ads can count as "sharing" your data under those laws. The settings above are how you opt out.

Who gets your data

I don't sell your personal data or give it to anyone for their own marketing. It goes only to the services that run the game:

  • Google: ads and consent (AdMob and its consent tool), and payments and purchase records (Google Play Billing). Google's privacy policy.
  • Ad partners working through AdMob, as described under Ads. They're listed in the consent form, or email me for the current list.
  • Unity Technologies: the game is built with Unity, and Unity In-App Purchasing connects it to Google Play Billing. Unity's privacy policy.
  • Authorities, only if the law requires it.

If the game were ever sold or transferred, this policy would go with it. The new owner would have to keep it or tell you before changing it.

Transfers outside Europe

Google, Unity and AdMob's ad partners may process your data in the United States and other countries outside the European Economic Area. These transfers are protected by the EU–US Data Privacy Framework where the company takes part in it, and otherwise by the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses.

How long it's kept

  • Game data and settings on your phone: until you uninstall the game or clear its data.
  • Purchase records: as long as tax and accounting law requires. In Spain that can be up to six years. Google keeps its own records as the seller.
  • Ad data: for the periods set by Google and its ad partners in their own privacy policies.

Your rights

You can ask me to:

  • Access: get a copy of the data I hold about you.
  • Correct it if it's wrong.
  • Delete it. See below.
  • Restrict or object to how it's used, including anything based on legitimate interest.
  • Take it with you in a common format (portability).
  • Withdraw consent at any time — for ads with the Privacy button in Settings, for reminders with the notifications switch. This doesn't affect anything done before you withdrew.

Because the game has no accounts, I don't hold a profile about you: your game data lives on your phone, and ad data is held by Google and its partners. Email rafaelrivero6240@gmail.com and I'll help with any request, including passing it on to Google where needed. I'll reply within one month. It's free, and I won't treat you differently for asking.

If you're unhappy with my answer, you can complain to Spain's data protection authority, the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos (AEPD), or to the authority where you live.

Delete your data

There's no account to delete, so removing your data is in your hands:

  1. Uninstall the game, or clear its storage in Android's settings. This removes your scores, seeds and settings.
  2. Delete or reset your advertising ID in Android's settings, so ad data can no longer be linked to your phone.
  3. To ask Google about the ad and purchase data it holds, use your Google account's privacy settings.
  4. Questions? Email rafaelrivero6240@gmail.com with the subject "Sugoi Fruit Merge — my data".

Some records aren't deleted: Google keeps its own records of any purchases, and I keep purchase order numbers for as long as tax law requires. Deleting your data doesn't refund purchases, and seeds you bought are lost when the game's data is cleared.

Children

Sugoi Fruit Merge is a general-audience puzzle game. It isn't made for young children and isn't aimed at anyone under 13. In the EU, personalised ads need consent, and a child under the age of digital consent in their country can't give it alone: that age is 14 in Spain, and between 13 and 16 elsewhere in the EU. Those players need permission from a parent or guardian.

If you're a parent and think your child has shared data through the game, email me and I'll help you remove it.

Security

Data travels to Google over encrypted connections. No system is perfectly secure, but the game only uses what it needs and keeps your game data on your own phone, which limits what could ever be exposed.

Changes to this policy

If the game starts handling data differently, I'll update this page and its date. If a change is significant, I'll also tell you in the game before it takes effect.
